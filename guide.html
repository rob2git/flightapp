Getting started

Flightapp shows live status for the flights you choose to track: gate and terminal, delays, estimated and actual times, how far along the flight is, and a map once it is in the air.

The first time you open it, it runs in Demo mode with four simulated flights, so you can see how the cards behave. Nothing is sent anywhere in Demo mode. The simulated times are set relative to the moment you first opened the app.

To see real flights, you need a data provider and a key (see Connecting real data). Open Settings (the sliders icon in the top bar), pick a provider, paste your key and tap Save. The example flights are removed when you switch to a real provider, and your own flights are looked up again.

Adding a flight

Tap Track flight and fill in four things. Together they pick out exactly one flight.

  • Flight number: the airline code and number, such as BA286 or BA 286. Use the code printed on the ticket (up to 4 digits).
  • Departure date: the date the flight leaves, in the departure airport's local time. For a late-night departure, use the date shown at the airport you leave from, not the date where you are.
  • From and To: the 3-letter IATA airport codes, such as SFO or LHR. Typing shows suggestions for the airports the app knows.

A number with stops (for example one flight number that goes A to B to C) is shared by every leg. The two airports tell the app which leg you mean, so enter the airports for your own leg.

Flight numbers are reused day after day, which is why the date matters. If you try to add the same flight twice, the app tells you that you are already tracking it.

To remove a flight, open Details on its card and tap Remove. Settings > Remove all flights clears the whole list (tap twice to confirm).

Reading a flight card

From top to bottom, each card shows:

  • Header: the flight number, the airline and date, and the status label on the right (see below).
  • Route: the two airport codes with their cities. If a city is not known, the airport's name is shown instead. A dashed arc joins them, with a small plane partway along it while the flight is in the air.
  • Departure and arrival tiles: the time in each airport's own local time, shown on flip-board style tiles, with the date and time zone underneath.
    • The small label above the time says whether it is Scheduled, Estimated, or an actual time (Departed or Landed).
    • If the time has moved, the original is shown as "was" with the old time struck through, plus a coloured chip such as +25 min (late), 10 min early or On time.
    • A small +1 next to an arrival time means it lands on the next calendar day (local time at the destination).
    • Terminal, gate and (for arrivals) baggage belt appear when the provider has them. Before that you see "Terminal and gate not published yet". Airlines often assign gates only a few hours before departure.
  • Summary line: for example "Departs in 1 h 35 min", "Lands in 5 h 30 min, 52% of the way" or "Landed 40 min ago".
  • Map: a collapsible section for flights that are in the air or have landed (see Maps).
  • Details: aircraft type and registration, distance, altitude, ground speed, scheduled block time (gate to gate), the data source, the time of the last update, and the Refresh and Remove buttons. Altitude and ground speed need a live position, so they show "Not in flight" otherwise.

The cards are listed by departure time, earliest first. A landed or cancelled flight keeps its place for 24 hours after arrival, then moves to the bottom of the list, most recent first. The line above the cards counts your flights, how many are in the air or waiting, and when the next one departs.

What the status labels mean

The label combines what the provider reports with the actual times, so it will not say "Departed" while the arrival card says "Landed". Delays of 15 minutes or more turn the label orange.

Flights in the future

You can add a flight well before its day. It is saved, and the card says Tracking starts followed by the date. No lookups are made until that date begins, so a flight added weeks ahead uses none of your plan's quota.

"Begins" means 00:00 on the departure date in the departure airport's time zone. If the airport is not in the app's built-in list, your device's time zone is used. At that moment the app looks the flight up once, and the card turns into a normal one. Tapping refresh on a waiting card does nothing, because the app will not call the provider before then.

After that first lookup, updates follow the rules in Refreshing and saving API calls.

Maps

Once a flight is in the air or has landed, its card gets a Map section. It is open by default while the flight is airborne, and the app remembers whether you opened or closed it for as long as the app stays open.

  • The dashed line is the planned route (the shortest path over the Earth, which looks curved on a flat map).
  • The solid line is the part already flown, and the plane marks where the aircraft is.
  • The caption says where the position came from: the provider, or "estimated from the schedule" when no live position is available. An estimated position moves along the route as time passes.

The map is drawn by the app itself, so it works offline and needs no map service. Three things to know:

  • The map needs both airports to be in the app's built-in list of about 140 major airports. If one is missing, that flight simply has no map.
  • With FlightAware, the aircraft sits at its last reported position, and the solid line runs straight from the origin to the aircraft rather than the exact path. This saves one lookup per refresh.
  • With AeroDataBox and AviationStack, the aircraft sits at the position the provider reports. They do not supply a flown path, and some plans give no live position at all.

Connecting real data: providers and keys

Flightapp does not have its own flight data. It asks a flight-data provider that you sign up with, using your own key. Pick one in Settings. Each provider keeps its own key and proxy address in the app, so you can switch back and forth without retyping.

Which one should I choose?

  • Just trying it: stay in Demo mode.
  • Richest card (gates, baggage, aircraft): AeroDataBox or FlightAware.
  • Lowest cost to start: compare each provider's current free or entry plan. Offers change, so check their sites for today's quotas and prices.
  • Flights well in advance: any provider works, because Flightapp waits for the day before it makes a request. FlightAware itself only answers up to 2 days ahead.

Getting a key

The steps are similar for each provider. Sign-up pages and plan names change, so use the provider's own instructions for the details.

  1. Create an account with the provider and choose a plan. Subscribe to the specific API named below if the provider asks.
  2. Copy the key it gives you.
  3. In Flightapp, open Settings, pick the same provider, paste the key into API key and tap Save.
  • AeroDataBox via RapidAPI: go to rapidapi.com, search for "AeroDataBox", subscribe, and copy your RapidAPI key.
  • AeroDataBox via API.Market: go to api.market, find AeroDataBox, subscribe, and copy your key.
  • FlightAware AeroAPI: go to flightaware.com, search the site for "AeroAPI", choose a plan, and create a key in the AeroAPI portal.
  • AviationStack: go to aviationstack.com, sign up and copy your access key.

The app's requests go over a secure (HTTPS) connection. If a provider refuses one for your plan, the card says so.

The proxy address (optional)

Some providers or plans do not allow requests straight from a web page. The sign is the message "Could not reach the provider" even though your key is right and you are online. The fix is a small relay, called a proxy, which you put in Settings > Proxy address. The project's README explains how to set one up (the file is proxy/worker.js). Leave the box empty otherwise.

What changes when you switch providers

When you save a different provider, key or proxy, the app clears the stored statuses and looks every flight up again once. Flights in the future stay saved and still wait for their date. The built-in example flights are removed when you move from Demo to a real provider.

Refreshing and saving API calls

Most providers limit how many requests you can make, and some charge per request. Flightapp is built to use as few as possible, and by default it only updates when you ask.

Ways to refresh yourself

  • The circular-arrow button in the top bar refreshes every flight.
  • Refresh in a card's Details refreshes just that flight.
  • Adding a flight looks it up straight away (unless it is in the future).
  • Saving a change of provider, key or proxy looks all flights up again.

The "Refresh during a flight" setting

SettingWhat happens by itself
Only when I tap refresh (default)Nothing. After the first lookup, the app only calls the provider when you tap refresh. Best for saving quota.
Every 2, 5 or 15 minutesThat interval from 3 hours before departure until 1 hour after arrival. In the 24 hours before that, every 30 minutes. Earlier than that, every 6 hours.

With either setting, the app stops refreshing by itself once a flight has landed, been cancelled or been diverted. You can still tap refresh at any time.

When no request is made

  • Flights waiting for their date.
  • While the app is hidden or in the background, or while you are offline. When you come back, or the connection returns, it checks straight away.
  • The countdowns, progress arc and "in the air" estimates update every 20 seconds from information already on the device. That uses no requests.

If a lookup fails

The card keeps the last good information and adds a note that it is showing the last known status.

  • Wrong or missing key: no automatic retries until you fix it in Settings.
  • Flight not found, or quota reached: the app waits for your tap in "Only when I tap refresh" mode. With an interval set, it tries again after an hour.
  • No answer or another error: it tries again after about 10 minutes.

Tips for staying within a quota

  • Keep the default, and refresh when you actually want to know.
  • Add flights ahead of time. Waiting flights cost nothing until their day.
  • Remove flights you no longer need. A tracked flight with an interval set keeps refreshing until it lands.
  • Use 15 minutes rather than 2 if you want automatic updates.

Installing and using it offline

Flightapp is a web app you can install so it opens like any other app, in its own window and with its own icon. The page needs to be served over HTTPS (or run from localhost).

  • iPhone or iPad (Safari): tap the Share button, then Add to Home Screen.
  • Android (Chrome): open the menu and choose Install app (or Add to Home screen).
  • Computer (Chrome or Edge): use the install icon at the right of the address bar, or the browser menu's Install option.

Once it has loaded, the app opens without a connection. Offline, you see the last known status of each flight, and an Offline label appears in the top bar. Times, countdowns and estimated map positions keep moving, but live data returns only when you are back online.

If you ever see an old version, close and reopen the app once. It checks for a new version every time it loads.

Privacy and where things are stored

  • There is no Flightapp account. Your flights, settings and keys are stored in this browser on this device, and nowhere else.
  • Your key and flight details are sent only to the provider you chose. If you set a proxy address, the request passes through that proxy on its way to the provider.
  • The keys are stored as plain text in the browser's local storage. Anyone who can use this browser profile could find them, so avoid saving keys on shared computers.
  • Clearing the site's data in your browser removes your flights and keys. The same data is not shared between browsers or devices: each one has its own list.
  • In Demo mode nothing leaves the device.
